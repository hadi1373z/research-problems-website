1. Main draft theorem

Fix a finite-depth scalar-output network built from normalized partial-matching tensor operators, channel mixing, constant biases and Lipschitz pointwise activations. Tensor orders, widths, weights and depth are independent of sample size. Section 2 defines the exact class, including which diagonal operations it omits.

Let Nn be its output on a simple loopless Bernoulli graph sampled from any symmetric measurable graphon W, with bounded measurable signal f. There is a deterministic sampling limit μ = μN(W,f). If K is the largest tensor order and n,m ≥ 2K, then

E|Nn − μ| ≤ PL/√(n−K) + QL/n,
E|Nn − Nm| ≤ PL[(n−K)−1/2+(m−K)−1/2]
+ QL(1/n+1/m).

The constants below are explicit in the architecture, weights, activation constants and signal bound, and uniform over W and f in that bounded class. The second inequality follows by the triangle inequality; in particular it holds for independent samples. No polynomial expansion or graphon smoothness is used.

What advances beyond the initial notebook

The previous local-kernel bound stopped before a nonlinear activation after pooling. The proof here replaces each entire intermediate tensor by a bounded function of a tuple’s latent positions and internal edge bits. Conditional averaging then carries the estimate through every fixed layer. Section 8 gives a genuinely nonlocal three-layer ReLU example with a matching root-n obstruction.

Section 6 also gives an explicit recursion for continuous activation moduli and the worst-case sharp Hölder exponent ½∏αℓ. Neither claim supplies one universal rate for all continuous activations.

2. Exact model and constants

Draw X1,…,Xn independently uniformly on [0,1]. Independently draw one uniform Uij for each unordered pair i<j, put Aij=Aji=1{Uij≤W(Xi,Xj)}, and set Aii=0. Assume |f|≤R. The order-two input has channels H0,1(i,j)=Aij and H0,2(i,j)=f(Xi).

A basis map γ from order p to order q is a bijection between a subset of s input positions and a subset of s output positions. The other rγ=p−s input positions are free. For each output tuple i∈[n]q,

(Tγ,nH)(i) = n−rγ Σj∈[n]rγ H(v(i,j)).

Here matched input positions copy their matched output indices, and free positions take j in a fixed order. All free index tuples, including repetitions, are summed. Orders zero are allowed. These maps average, permute and replicate; they do not extract within-input diagonals or multiply by output equality masks. The theorem is for this partial-matching class, not the full partition basis of arbitrary finite IGNs.

Hℓ,a = σℓ(bℓ,a + Σb,γwℓ,a,b,γTγ,nHℓ−1,b),
k0=2, kL=0, Nn=HL,1.

Take the last width to be one; its activation may be the identity. Write K=max kℓ, aℓ=maxaΣb,γ|wℓ,a,b,γ| and bℓ=maxa|bℓ,a|. Let rℓ be the largest free order of a nonzero-weight map (zero if none), and dℓ=kℓrℓ+rℓ(rℓ−1)/2. Let Lℓ be a Lipschitz constant on the reachable preactivation interval.

Every T has sup norm at most one. Thus M0=max(1,R), and any uniform bounds Mℓ for the finite tensors and the limiting fields may be used. One valid choice is

Mℓ = |σℓ(0)| + Lℓ(bℓ+aℓMℓ−1).

With P0=Q0=0, the theorem’s constants are

Pℓ = Lℓaℓ(Pℓ−1+Mℓ−1rℓ),
Qℓ = Lℓaℓ(Qℓ−1+2Mℓ−1dℓ).

For continuous activations use Mℓ=sup{|σℓ(z)|: |z|≤bℓ+aℓMℓ−1}. Boundedness then follows from compactness at each fixed layer. Constants can be coarse and may grow rapidly with depth.

3. The conditional-tuple sampling limit

For an injective ordered k-tuple I, its type τI consists of the latent positions (Xi)i∈I and every internal Bernoulli edge bit between these vertices. Signals are f at these positions. Denote the space of such abstract types by Typesk; Types0 is a singleton. Fields below are bounded measurable functions on these spaces.

The limiting partial-matching operator

Given an output type τ∈Typesq, retain the s matched latent positions and their mutual edge bits. Draw rγ fresh independent uniform latent positions. Draw all input edges involving at least one fresh position as conditionally independent Bernoulli variables with probabilities W. Form the resulting p-type, in the input order prescribed by γ. For a bounded input field φ define

(ℙγφ)(τ) = E[φ(fresh input type) | τ].

Only the matched output vertices occur in the input type. Conditioning on the full output type, including unused output vertices, causes no difficulty. The fresh latent positions and required edge uniforms are independent of that conditioning. This explicit construction defines ℙ even on output types having probability zero.

Initialize φ0,1(x1,x2,e12)=e12 and φ0,2=f(x1). Recursively set

φℓ,a(τ) = σℓ(bℓ,a + Σb,γwℓ,a,b,γℙγφℓ−1,b(τ)).

Integration preserves bounded measurability, and |ℙγφ|≤||φ||∞. Induction gives |φℓ,a|≤Mℓ. At order zero the type is empty, so φL,1 is the deterministic number μ in the theorem.

Why retaining Bernoulli bits matters

The limit need not be the original network evaluated directly on the weighted graphon W. A nonlinear local expression Aij2 equals Aij on a simple graph. Its normalized mean tends to ∫W, whereas the corresponding weighted expression gives ∫W2. A ReLU realization is ρ(2Aij−1). This distinction survives at large n.

The present result compares sizes sampled from the same (W,f) through their common Bernoulli sampling limit. It does not establish cut-distance continuity of the original weighted-input network, transfer between different graphons, or trained-model generalization.

4. Conditional averaging lemma and proof

Lemma. Fix a partial matching from p to q with r free input positions, n≥p+q, and M≥0. Let Hn be any bounded, possibly globally data-dependent input tensor, and φ a local p-type field, both bounded in absolute value by M. Suppose for every injective input tuple I, E|Hn(I)−φ(τI)|≤e. Then for every injective output tuple B,

E|Tγ,nHn(B) − ℙγφ(τB)|
≤ e + Mr/√(n−q) + (2M/n)[qr+r(r−1)/2].

1. Remove collisions explicitly

Call a free tuple good if its r indices are distinct and avoid all q output vertices. Good tuples have mass g=(n−q)r/nr, where (a)r is the falling factorial. Under independent uniform free indices, a union bound gives

1−g ≤ [qr+r(r−1)/2]/n.

For good tuples the assembled input is injective. Replacing Hn by φ in their normalized average costs at most ge≤e in unconditional expected absolute error. This step uses no independence of Hn entries. The contribution from bad tuples has absolute value at most M(1−g).

2. Concentrate only the local field

Let U be the average of φ over good ordered free tuples, normalized to total mass one. Conditional on τB, each summand has mean h=ℙγφ(τB). Two summands using disjoint fresh vertex sets are conditionally independent: shared output latents and internal edges have been fixed, and the fresh latents and edge uniforms actually used by the two kernels are disjoint. Unused cross-edges play no role.

For two independent uniformly chosen good free tuples, the probability their fresh sets overlap is at most r2/(n−q): each of the r2 pairs of positions agrees with probability 1/(n−q). Every remaining conditional covariance has absolute value at most M2, by Cauchy–Schwarz and the bounded range. Consequently

Var(U | τB) ≤ M2r2/(n−q),
E|U−h| ≤ Mr/√(n−q).

For r=0 interpret U=h and the fluctuation term as zero, including the boundary n=q; there are no collisions. For r>0, n≥p+q guarantees that good tuples exist. Finally compare the good part gU plus the bad part to h: its error is bounded by g|U−h|+2M(1−g). Adding the earlier replacement error proves the lemma.

Dependence audit: applying conditional concentration directly to the actual hidden tensor would be unjustified: it depends on the entire graph. The unconditional H-to-φ comparison precedes the conditional variance calculation, which is applied only to the local field φ.

5. Induction and a concentration corollary

For each layer, let eℓ(n) be the maximum over channels and injective output tuples of E|Hℓ,a(I)−φℓ,a(τI)|. Exchangeability makes the tuple choice immaterial, but a maximum suffices. Initially e0=0. Since n≥2K, the lemma applies to every basis map. Its q is kℓ≤K, while r≤rℓ.

Apply the triangle inequality to channel mixing and then the Lipschitz activation. Both preactivations lie in the same bounded interval. Monotonicity of qr+r(r−1)/2 in r gives

eℓ(n) ≤ Lℓaℓ[eℓ−1(n)
+ Mℓ−1rℓ/√(n−K) + 2Mℓ−1dℓ/n].

Insert the definitions of Pℓ and Qℓ from Section 2 to obtain eℓ(n)≤Pℓ/√(n−K)+Qℓ/n by induction. At order zero this is precisely the first theorem bound. Comparing both scalar outputs to μ proves the second bound. No step approximates σ by a polynomial.

High-probability form for Lipschitz layers

Let Λ=∏ℓ=1LLℓaℓ. Each T is a contraction in normalized entrywise L¹: average |TH| over all output tuples, use the triangle inequality, and note that matched output indices and free indices together are independent uniform input coordinates. Replicated unused output positions cancel. Thus the network is Λ-Lipschitz in the maximum-channel normalized L¹ input distance.

Resampling one latent Xi changes the adjacency only in one row and column and the signal only in one first-coordinate slice. Its output effect is at most 2M0Λ/n. Resampling one unordered edge uniform changes two entries, giving effect at most 2Λ/n2. These X and U variables are independent. Their squared bounded differences sum to at most Λ2(4M02+2)/n. McDiarmid’s inequality therefore gives, for Λ>0,

Pr(|Nn−ENn|≥t) ≤ 2 exp{−nt2/[Λ2(2M02+1)]}.

Combining with |ENn−μ|≤E|Nn−μ| yields, with probability at least 1−δ,

|Nn−μ| ≤ PL/√(n−K)+QL/n
+Λ√[(2M02+1)log(2/δ)/n].

If Λ=0, some layer is constant with respect to the preceding tensors, so the final output is deterministic and sample-size independent. The probability statement is then interpreted directly, without division by zero.

6. Continuous moduli and Hölder rates

For continuous σℓ, put Zℓ=bℓ+aℓMℓ−1. Let ωℓ(t) be its modulus of continuity on [−Zℓ,Zℓ], constant beyond that interval’s diameter. Define

Ωℓ(t)=infδ>0(1+t/δ)ωℓ(δ), t>0; Ωℓ(0)=0.

Subdividing a segment into steps of length at most δ shows ωℓ(t)≤(1+t/δ)ωℓ(δ). Hence Ω majorizes ω. It is nondecreasing and concave as an infimum of affine functions of t. Taking δ=t gives Ω(t)≤2ω(t), so it tends to zero at zero, and the extension there remains concave. Jensen’s inequality therefore applies to Ω.

Write ηℓ(n)=Mℓ−1[rℓ/√(n−K)+2dℓ/n]. With u0(n)=0 define

uℓ(n)=Ωℓ(aℓ[uℓ−1(n)+ηℓ(n)]).

The lemma bounds the mean preactivation discrepancy by aℓ(eℓ−1+ηℓ). The pointwise discrepancy is controlled by ω, then Ω; Jensen and monotonicity give eℓ≤uℓ inductively. Thus E|Nn−Nm|≤uL(n)+uL(m). Every fixed finite-depth network converges, with an explicit rate once its activation moduli are supplied.

Closed form for Hölder activations

Suppose |σℓ(x)−σℓ(y)|≤Hℓ|x−y|αℓ on the reachable interval, with 0<αℓ≤1. Set β0=1/2 and C0=0. For n≥2K define constants

βℓ=αℓβℓ−1, Tℓ=√2 rℓ+2dℓ,
Cℓ=Hℓ{aℓ[Cℓ−1+Mℓ−1Tℓ]}αℓ.

Then E|Nn−μ|≤CLn−βL, where βL=½∏αℓ. Indeed ηℓ≤Mℓ−1Tℓn−1/2, βℓ−1≤1/2, and Jensen for tαℓ gives the displayed recursion. Setting all αℓ=1 recovers a root-n power; the earlier P,Q separate its lower-order collision correction.

7. The Hölder exponent is sharp in this class

This is a worst-case statement over networks in the specified class, not a claim about every fixed architecture. It also separates activation regularity from graphon smoothness.

Take W≡0 and f=+1 on half of [0,1], −1 on the other half. A normalized integration of the replicated signal gives the sample mean Sn=n−1Σif(Xi). Use scalar-output activations σℓ(t)=|t|αℓ successively; between them use identity linear maps. Each activation is globally αℓ-Hölder with constant one. If needed, an identity final readout has exponent one.

Nn=|Sn|A, A=∏αℓ, μ=0.

For independent equal-size samples Sn and S′n, the two-dimensional central limit theorem gives (√n Sn,√n S′n) converging in distribution to independent standard normal variables Z,Z′. The transformed differences are uniformly integrable: 0<A≤1 and the second moments of the normalized sample means are one, so a power greater than one of the differences has a uniform bound. Therefore

nA/2E||Sn|A−|S′n|A|
→ E||Z|A−|Z′|A| > 0.

The limiting expectation is finite and positive because |Z|A is nonconstant. This proves that the exponent A/2 in Section 6 cannot be improved uniformly over this network class. In particular no o(n−1/2) equal-size transfer rate holds uniformly for Lipschitz activations.

What this lower bound does and does not use

This construction uses signal fluctuations, not edge randomness. Section 9 supplies a separate three-layer example whose root-n bias comes entirely from graph edges and a nonlinear threshold after common-neighbor pooling. Both are fixed networks: parameters do not change with n.

If an activation has only continuity and no prescribed modulus, Section 6 gives a modulus-dependent recursion, but no common power follows. This is consistent with the earlier notebook construction of arbitrarily slow continuous networks. The new bound refines that observation by identifying the sharp power when Hölder regularity is specified.

8. A genuinely nonlocal three-layer network

Let ρ(t)=max(t,0), fix θ∈[0,1], and define the order sequence 2→3→2→0:

H1(i,j,k)=ρ(Aik+Ajk−1)=AikAjk,
Cij=n−1ΣkH1(i,j,k),
H2(i,j)=ρ(Cij−θ),
Nn=n−2Σi,jH2(i,j).

This has three affine layers, two ReLUs, and an identity readout. The middle activation acts after a global sum over k, so it lies outside the previous fixed local-kernel argument. Every operator is a partial matching with the required normalization, including repeated tuples and Aii=0.

q(x,y)=∫01W(x,z)W(y,z)dz,
μθ=∫[0,1]²(q(x,y)−θ)+ dxdy.

A sharper direct bound: for every W and n≥2,

E|Nn−μθ| ≤ (3/2−θ)/√n + (3−θ)/n.

Proof with all finite-size terms

For i≠j, condition on Xi,Xj. The n−2 terms AikAjk, k∉{i,j}, are independent Bernoulli(q(Xi,Xj)); terms k=i,j vanish. The conditional standard deviation of Cij is at most √(n−2)/(2n)≤1/(2√n), and its conditional mean differs from q by at most 2/n. Since ρ is 1-Lipschitz, replacing every off-diagonal H2 by h(Xi,Xj)=(q−θ)+ costs at most 1/(2√n)+2/n in expected normalized error.

Write B=1−θ. The injective ordered-pair average U of h is unbiased for μθ, with 0≤h≤B. Disjoint pairs are independent. A pair of uniformly selected injective ordered pairs shares a vertex with probability at most 4/n, and each covariance is at most B2/4. Hence Var(U)≤B2/n and E|U−μθ|≤B/√n.

The off-diagonal fraction is (n−1)/n; the diagonal contribution is (1/n)D with 0≤D≤B. Thus replacing the ideal full expression by μ costs at most B/√n+B/n. Adding the two estimates proves the bound. Applying it at n and m gives the corresponding transfer inequality.

9. Matching root-n bias from graph edges

Take a constant graphon W≡p with 0<p<1 and choose θ=p2. Then q≡p2 and μθ=0. For i≠j, nCij has distribution Bin(n−2,p2); for i=j, nCii is the degree, with distribution Bin(n−1,p). Therefore the exact expectation is

ENn = [(n−1)/n2] E[(Bin(n−2,p2)−nθ)+]
+ [1/n2] E[(Bin(n−1,p)−nθ)+].

The diagonal term is O(1/n). For the first term, the centered binomial central limit theorem applies; the shift between (n−2)p2 and nθ is constant and vanishes after division by √n. Uniformly bounded second moments of the normalized variables justify passing to expected positive parts. Consequently

√n ENn → √[p2(1−p2)/(2π)] > 0.

For independent sizes n and m=n2, E|Nn−Nn²|≥|ENn−ENn²| is asymptotic from below to this positive constant divided by √n. Section 8 gives the matching upper order. Thus even this nonlinear common-neighbor network has a worst-case transfer obstruction of order n−1/2.

Separately construct the conditional-type operators on the equal-mass two-block graphon W=[[1/4,3/4],[3/4,1/2]]. All 3,544 conditional rows sum to one exactly using rational arithmetic.
