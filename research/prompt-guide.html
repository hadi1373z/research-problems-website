What the prompt asks the researcher to do

  1. Refresh the live revision. Read the selected problem, previous attempts, relevant full proofs and decisive primary sources. Summaries are navigation aids, not proof evidence.
  2. Select one ready task matching the user’s latest direction. State a precise target and what would count as a proof, counterexample or useful obstruction. Explain how it advances the remaining gap.
  3. Audit the specific baseline lemma the new argument needs. If it is still unproved, label the dependent conclusion conditional. A repeated self-check is not independent review.
  4. Compare the target with the closest primary results: match hypotheses, conclusion and quantitative range, with exact theorem/section links and dates. A known special case is a rederivation, not evidence of publication novelty.
  5. Do substantive research and produce a concise, checkable proof or exact counterexample. If the first route fails, use the obstruction to choose a justified alternative. Do not spend the session mostly restyling the website or repeating an earlier result.
  6. Try to falsify the strongest step. Check boundary cases, normalization, hidden labeling and global-versus-local consistency. Use a genuinely different exact computation when useful; keep finite evidence separate from a general proof.
  7. For computations, record input provenance/hash, complete versus sampled range, exact versus numerical arithmetic and the commands actually run. Compare the tested range with existing literature before calling it an advance.
  8. State what survived, failed or remains conditional. If no reliable delta survives, record no-progress with the exact obstruction and a sharper next test. Never manufacture a result or raise review status to satisfy the request.
  9. Save the mathematical delta, evidence, prior-work comparison, limitations and a concrete next task. Preserve historical attempts. Publish only within the user’s authorization; otherwise return a reviewable draft.

What counts as a useful outcome

  • A theorem must state exact hypotheses and give a complete public proof of its new step, with dependencies and unresolved lemmas identified.
  • A counterexample must give exact objects and verify every hypothesis and the failed conclusion. Identify whether it refutes a method, restricted information claim or the full conjecture.
  • A computational contribution must have reproducible code/data and accurately bounded scope. More small examples alone do not establish an unrestricted theorem or novelty.
  • An audit must identify what was checked and by whom. The same assistant’s second pass is a self-audit. Literature updates need a primary theorem and a hypothesis comparison.
  • A polished explanation, a larger session count or a prompt/UI change alone is not a mathematical contribution. An honest, specific obstruction is an acceptable outcome.

Keep the evidence label accurate

A script checks a restricted certificate on eight-vertex cores; full reconstruction was already checked through order 13.

Report an exact restricted-certificate audit and its new coverage; do not claim a new full reconstruction bound.

The original assistant rereads its own proof and reruns its own program.

Record self-audit and keep needs-independent-review.

Two graphs agree on three moments and one card but their full decks were not compared.

At most refute that information-based method; do not report a counterexample to graph reconstruction.

A primary paper proves an equivalent theorem under the same hypotheses.

Attribute it, record a rederivation or audit, and choose a genuinely unresolved next target.

The final handoff should contain

  • Outcome and exact delta from the refreshed baseline.
  • Precise claim, assumptions and a proof/evidence artifact.
  • Closest prior work, comparison and novelty status.
  • Falsification checks actually executed, including failures and computational scope.
  • Remaining limitations, dependencies and review status.
  • Saved artifact links and one next claim with an acceptance condition.

Why these instructions

Official OpenAI guidance recommends clear goals, constraints, an output contract and a definition of done for research workflows. It also recommends keeping prompts versioned and testing representative cases. This site applies those ideas to mathematical research; the mathematical review rules are notebook policy.

Versioned structural/contract checks are included. These do not measure future model research quality or guarantee a breakthrough. A page view does not start an AI job or grant permission to publish. The AI session still needs the user’s request, source access and appropriate tools.
