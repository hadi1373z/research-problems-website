Does efficient testing imply efficient distance estimation?

Model

Fix a graph property P in the dense model. qP(ε) counts sampled vertices; dP(G) is normalized edit distance. An estimator distinguishes dP(G) ≤ α from dP(G) ≥ α+ε, for every threshold α, with probability at least 2/3.

Full question

If P is testable using qP(ε) sampled vertices, is it estimable using qP(ε′) vertices for some ε′ = poly(ε)? The polynomial rescaling may depend on P, but not on n or ε.

Literature snapshot

The 2025 survey's Theorem 5.3 gives a general doubly exponential conversion. Problem 5.4 asks for the much smaller rescaled tester bound.

Literature checked: 2026-10-31. Explicit open problem in the survey; subsequent-work searches found no resolution. This is a literature review, not an automatic status guarantee. A research snapshot from 31 October 2026 is now recorded below; no unrestricted resolution is claimed.

Remaining gap

The polynomial rescaling conversion for arbitrary graph properties is unproved. The edgeless calculation establishes a necessary loss, not a counterexample to the stated conjecture.

Is graph isomorphism fixed-parameter tractable in maximum degree?

Model

Both input graphs have n vertices and maximum degree at most d, with d≥2 (degrees 0 and 1 are elementary). Fixed-parameter tractability means time f(d) · n^C for a computable f and an absolute constant C independent of d.

Full question

Is there an isomorphism algorithm with running time f(d) · n^C for all n and d?

Literature snapshot

Grohe, Neuen and Schweitzer obtain n^{O((log d)^c)} time for an absolute c. The exponent still depends on d; Neuen's 2026 survey, §4.1 after Corollary 4.7, explicitly records FPT in maximum degree as open.

Literature checked: 2026-10-31. Explicit open-status statement in the survey; no later FPT resolution was located. A research snapshot from 31 October 2026 is now recorded below; no unrestricted resolution is claimed.

Remaining gap

An FPT algorithm in the number of branching vertices does not imply FPT in maximum degree: a cubic graph may have every vertex branching.

Does the vertex-deleted deck determine every graph?

Model

G is a finite simple undirected graph on n>=3 vertices. Its deck is the complete multiset of n unlabeled vertex-deleted induced subgraphs G-v. Repeated isomorphism types retain multiplicity; the vertex correspondence between cards is hidden.

Full question

A draft certificate for two leaves over a symmetric core

needs-independent-review · novelty: unassessed

For G=H plus leaves a-x and b-y, n=h+2, the full deck recovers the unordered endpoint vertex-orbits and every simple-path count p_l^H(x,y), 0<=l<=h-2. If this signature identifies one unordered-pair orbit under Aut(H), G is reconstructible among all graphs with its deck.

Assumptions
  • Finite simple undirected graphs; the full multiset of unlabeled one-vertex-deleted induced cards is given, including multiplicities.
  • The restricted model is a connected core H of order h with minimum degree at least two, plus leaves directly attached to core vertices. Recognition against arbitrary full-deck mates is part of the proof.
  • Path subgraph counts are ordinary, not induced; endpoint paths in H are vertex-simple. Only proper patterns with fewer than n vertices are recovered by Kelly counting.
  • The two attachment vertices may coincide. Signature separation is a stated hypothesis, not a universal theorem.
Argument

Recover edge count and each deleted degree; leaf cards expose H and force any deck-mate to be the same type of leaf extension. Kelly counting gives c(P_k,G). Inclusion-exclusion c(P_k,G)-c(P_k,G-a)-c(P_k,G-b)+c(P_k,H) counts paths containing both leaves, which must be endpoints, hence equals p_(k-3)^H(x,y). A unique pair orbit then yields an isomorphism.

Limitation

This extends the distance-only certificate in this notebook, not a claimed literature-first theorem. The anchor/endvertex framework is established. Separation has not been proved for arbitrary cores; automorphism and simple-path enumeration may be expensive. Independent review and novelty comparison are pending.

An explicit infinite family with nontrivial core symmetry

needs-independent-review · novelty: unassessed

Every graph obtained by attaching two leaves, allowing a shared base, to K_s box K_2 for s>=3 is reconstructed by the path certificate. The four pair signatures (p0,p1,p2) are (1,0,0), (0,1,s-2), (0,1,0), (0,0,2).

Assumptions
  • Finite simple undirected graphs; the full multiset of unlabeled one-vertex-deleted induced cards is given, including multiplicities.
  • The restricted model is a connected core H of order h with minimum degree at least two, plus leaves directly attached to core vertices. Recognition against arbitrary full-deck mates is part of the proof.
  • Path subgraph counts are ordinary, not induced; endpoint paths in H are vertex-simple. Only proper patterns with fewer than n vertices are recovered by Kelly counting.
  • H consists of two s-cliques joined by a perfect matching; s>=3.
Argument

Simultaneously permuting clique positions and swapping the two cliques acts transitively on each of the four listed pair types. Their path signatures differ, proving they are precisely the unordered-pair orbits. The general certificate applies using proper paths on at most five vertices.

Quadratic recovery of attachment multiplicities and a gap criterion

needs-independent-review · novelty: unassessed

For G=H(a) with r attached leaves, the leaf-card sum satisfies sum Q(b)=(r-2)Q(a)+r, Q(a)=sum a_v^2. If r!=2, any leaf card with exactly one coordinate of the inferred value (Q(a)-Q(b)-1)/2 restores a uniquely. In particular a positive occupancy with an absent predecessor suffices, and every r>h(h-1)/2 forces such a gap.

Assumptions
  • Finite simple undirected graphs; the full multiset of unlabeled one-vertex-deleted induced cards is given, including multiplicities.
  • The restricted model is a connected core H of order h with minimum degree at least two, plus leaves directly attached to core vertices. Recognition against arbitrary full-deck mates is part of the proof.
  • Path subgraph counts are ordinary, not induced; endpoint paths in H are vertex-simple. Only proper patterns with fewer than n vertices are recovered by Kelly counting.
  • There may be any number r>=1 of directly attached leaves, with nonnegative integer multiplicities a_v. The moment formula can be inverted only for r!=2.
Argument

Each card b=a-e_v occurs a_v times. Expanding the square gives the identity and the removed value q=(Q(a)-Q(b)+1)/2. A unique coordinate of value q-1 determines the completion. If no positive occupancy has an absent predecessor, all values 0..M occur; then r<=Mh-M(M+1)/2<=h(h-1)/2.

Limitation

The strict threshold is sharp only for forcing this elementary gap; it is not a lower bound on reconstructibility. Lauri and the endvertex literature already provide strong group-action criteria. No priority or improvement over those results is claimed. The case r=2 needs other information.

A fixed path cutoff cannot handle every large core

needs-independent-review · novelty: unassessed

For every fixed L>=0, two nonisomorphic two-leaf extensions of C_(2L+5), at attachment distances L+1 and L+2, have the same leaf cards, endpoint vertex-orbits and p0..pL. On the triangular prism, four nonisomorphic two-leaf extensions even have the same entire leaf-deletion deck.

Assumptions
  • Finite simple undirected graphs; the full multiset of unlabeled one-vertex-deleted induced cards is given, including multiplicities.
  • The restricted model is a connected core H of order h with minimum degree at least two, plus leaves directly attached to core vertices. Recognition against arbitrary full-deck mates is part of the proof.
  • Path subgraph counts are ordinary, not induced; endpoint paths in H are vertex-simple. Only proper patterns with fewer than n vertices are recovered by Kelly counting.
  • This compares restricted information: two leaf cards and a bounded path signature. It does not assert equality of the full vertex-deleted decks.
Argument

The cycle is vertex-transitive. Both chosen pair distances exceed L, so all the listed simple-path counts vanish. The distances differ and are invariant under cycle automorphisms, making the full graphs nonisomorphic. For the prism, all single-leaf attachments lie in one orbit while unordered pairs have four orbits.

These are counterexamples to truncated or restricted reconstruction methods, not counterexamples to the Kelly-Ulam conjecture. The full proper profile separates the cycle pairs.

Can equal-density cospectral graphons resist finite cospectral approximation?

Model

Full question

Exact complete-tripartite behavior

needs-independent-review · novelty: unassessed

For a complete tripartite core plus isolates, τ/n² = min(ab,ac,bc) and T/n³ = abc; ε-farness implies T ≥ ε^(3/2)n³.

Assumptions
  • One complete tripartite core, with all other vertices isolated.
Argument

A Latin-rectangle triangle packing supplies min(AB,AC,BC) edge-disjoint triangles. Multiplying ab,ac,bc yields (abc)² ≥ ε³.

A direct root-n sampling bound for local networks

needs-independent-review · novelty: unassessed

For a bounded ordered-k local kernel |h| ≤ M, E|N_n−N_m| ≤ Mk(n^(−1/2)+m^(−1/2)) + Mk(k−1)(n^(−1)+m^(−1)).

Assumptions
  • Fixed k and n,m ≥ k; normalized averages over all ordered tuples.
  • Independent simple loopless graph-signals sampled from one fixed graphon-signal.
  • The kernel depends only on the tuple’s internal edges and signals.
Argument

Injective tuple kernels on disjoint vertex sets are independent, so their average has variance at most M²k²/n. Noninjective tuples change the average by at most Mk(k−1)/n.

Limitation

The limit integrates Bernoulli edge randomness; it need not equal the same formula evaluated directly on W. The result does not cover a nonlinear activation after global pooling.

A draft root-n bound at arbitrary fixed Lipschitz depth

needs-independent-review · novelty: unassessed

For the normalized partial-matching IWN class, E|N_n-mu| <= P_L/sqrt(n-K)+Q_L/n. Hence E|N_n-N_m| <= P_L[(n-K)^(-1/2)+(m-K)^(-1/2)]+Q_L(1/n+1/m), uniformly over bounded graphon-signals. P_0=Q_0=0; P_l=L_l a_l(P_(l-1)+M_(l-1) r_l), Q_l=L_l a_l(Q_(l-1)+2M_(l-1)d_l), d_l=k_l r_l+r_l(r_l-1)/2.

Assumptions
  • Fixed finite depth, tensor orders, channel widths and weights, independently of sample size; scalar output, K=max tensor order and n,m>=2K.
  • Simple loopless symmetric Bernoulli graph samples from one arbitrary measurable graphon W; iid uniform latent positions and one independent edge uniform per unordered pair; bounded measurable signal |f|<=R.
  • Every linear map is a finite linear combination of normalized partial matchings: r free input indices are summed over all tuples with factor n^(-r), including repetitions. No within-input diagonal extraction or output equality masks.
  • Constant channel biases and pointwise activations with the specified Lipschitz or continuity modulus on bounded reachable intervals. The last activation may be the identity.
  • The deterministic limit integrates conditional Bernoulli tuple types; it need not equal the original weighted-input network N(W,f).
  • L_l is the layer activation Lipschitz constant; a_l is the maximum absolute weight row sum; r_l is the maximum free input order among nonzero weights; M_l bounds finite tensors and limiting fields.
Argument

Construct a bounded conditional tuple field at each layer. First replace the globally dependent hidden tensor by that local field in unconditional mean. Conditional on the entire output tuple type, kernels on disjoint fresh vertex sets are independent. Their averaging error is at most M r/sqrt(n-q), and collisions cost at most 2M[qr+r(r-1)/2]/n. Lipschitz induction gives the constants. An L1 contraction argument also yields a stated bounded-differences concentration corollary.

Limitation

This is a self-contained draft, not an independently checked or novelty-certified theorem. It excludes full diagonal partition-basis operations, sparse/unbounded sampling, sample-size-dependent parameters and distribution shift. Constants may be loose or grow rapidly with depth; it is not a runtime or learning guarantee.

Explicit continuous-modulus recursion and sharp Hölder powers

needs-independent-review · novelty: unassessed

For continuous activations, e_l(n) <= Omega_l(a_l[e_(l-1)(n)+eta_l(n)]), with eta_l=M_(l-1)[r_l/sqrt(n-K)+2d_l/n] and an explicit concave majorant Omega_l of the activation modulus. If layer l is alpha_l-Hölder, the rate is O(n^(-beta)+m^(-beta)), beta=(1/2) product_l alpha_l. This exponent is worst-case sharp within the stated class.

Assumptions
  • Fixed finite depth, tensor orders, channel widths and weights, independently of sample size; scalar output, K=max tensor order and n,m>=2K.
  • Simple loopless symmetric Bernoulli graph samples from one arbitrary measurable graphon W; iid uniform latent positions and one independent edge uniform per unordered pair; bounded measurable signal |f|<=R.
  • Every linear map is a finite linear combination of normalized partial matchings: r free input indices are summed over all tuples with factor n^(-r), including repetitions. No within-input diagonal extraction or output equality masks.
  • Constant channel biases and pointwise activations with the specified Lipschitz or continuity modulus on bounded reachable intervals. The last activation may be the identity.
  • The deterministic limit integrates conditional Bernoulli tuple types; it need not equal the original weighted-input network N(W,f).
  • For the Hölder conclusion, 0<alpha_l<=1 and a finite Hölder constant is specified for each activation on its reachable preactivation interval. Sharpness is over the class of architectures, not for every architecture.
Argument

Use Omega(t)=inf_(delta>0)(1+t/delta)omega(delta), Omega(0)=0; segment subdivision shows omega<=Omega<=2omega and the infimum is concave. Apply Jensen to the conditional-averaging bound. Hölder Jensen multiplies the exponent at each layer. For sharpness, integrate an iid Rademacher signal and successively apply |t|^(alpha_l): the output is |sample mean|^(product alpha_l); a central limit argument with uniform integrability gives a positive matching constant for independent equal-size transfer.

Limitation

The modulus-dependent recursion does not give a common rate for arbitrary continuous activations. Worst-case power sharpness does not establish optimal architecture-specific constants. Independent proof and novelty review are pending.

A nonlinear three-layer example with a matching root-n obstruction

needs-independent-review · novelty: unassessed

For H1(i,j,k)=ReLU(A_ik+A_jk-1), H2(i,j)=ReLU(n^(-1)sum_k H1-theta), N_n=n^(-2)sum_(i,j)H2, theta in [0,1], E|N_n-mu_theta| <= (3/2-theta)/sqrt(n)+(3-theta)/n. For W identically p and theta=p^2, sqrt(n) E N_n -> sqrt[p^2(1-p^2)/(2 pi)]>0. Sizes n and n^2 therefore have a matching root-n transfer obstruction.

Assumptions
  • Orders 2->3->2->0; three affine layers, two ReLUs and an identity readout; all ordered tuples included; A_ii=0.
  • Independent simple Bernoulli graph samples from one W; fixed theta in [0,1]. For sharpness choose 0<p<1, constant W=p, and theta=p^2.
  • mu_theta = integral_(x,y) (integral_z W(x,z)W(y,z) dz-theta)_+ dxdy.
Argument

Condition on an off-diagonal pair: its common-neighbor sum is Bin(n-2,q), giving a 1/(2sqrt(n))+2/n approximation. The remaining bounded latent-pair U-statistic contributes (1-theta)/sqrt(n), with a (1-theta)/n diagonal correction. For constant W the exact expectation is a sum of two binomial positive-part expectations; the binomial central limit theorem gives the positive root-n constant.

Limitation

Finite exhaustive checks corroborate the explicit binomial identity, not the asymptotic proof. The lower bound uses unequal sizes n and n^2; the separate Rademacher example establishes equal-size sharpness across the general class. Independent review and novelty assessment remain pending.
